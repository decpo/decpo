Current research

Active Capturing Individual Differences in Sentence Processing

A-maze versus Self-Paced Reading Paradigms: Capturing Individual Differences in Sentence Processing

Two widely used paradigms in sentence processing, A-maze and self-paced reading, have recently been used to investigate individual differences in sentence processing. This project aims to compare the reliability of each task across two testing sessions.

Collaborators: Matt Robison, Department of Psychology at the University of Notre Dame

Active Associative Learning and Fluid Intelligence

Raven Predicts Learning Rate in Associative Learning, but Not in Free Recall

Investigating the relationship between fluid intelligence and learning across three tasks.

Collaborators: Matt Robison, Department of Psychology at the University of Notre Dame

Active Word Recognition in Children

Testing Predictive Effects of Distributed, Developmental Models of Word Recognition on Third-Grade Student Oral Reading Fluency (ORF) Error Profiles

A behavioral study evaluating decision-making accuracy when domain experts are presented with plausible but inaccurate algorithmic suggestions.

Lead Student: Natalie Gildea, CLAD Lab at the University of Notre Dame
